August 29, 2026

The One Thing

NVDA closed up 8.74% to $227.98 on nearly 293M shares traded — one of the most actively traded names in the entire market, not just a small mover. This is a name I actually hold.

Worth Watching

  • NVDA — that volume spike is the story. Whatever moved it is going to shape sentiment in every other chip name today, including INTC, also up notably (+4.36%).
  • Semiconductors broadly — INTC's move alongside NVDA suggests this isn't a single-stock story.
  • Crypto stayed open all weekend as usual, low drama by comparison.

Why This Matters

A single-name 8%+ move on this much volume usually isn't noise — it's either a real catalyst or a sector-wide rotation dragging the whole group with it. Today's the day to know which before reacting to it.

September 7, 2026

Markets are closed today for Labor Day, so Friday's tape is still the one that matters. The S&P 500 (SPY) closed Friday at $770.19, down 0.39% on the day — but only about 1% below the $777.88 close it put in on August 13, the high point of the past several months. That's a market sitting just off its own recent top, not one that spent the week cracking, even though the loudest thing that happened on the tape had nothing to do with the index at all.

Lululemon cut its full-year outlook for the second time this year Thursday evening, and the stock gapped down hard at Friday's open, trading between $97.99 and $103.16 before closing at $100.61 on 37.4 million shares — down 17.4% from Thursday's $121.77 close. The quarter itself was mixed, not uniformly bad: revenue of $2.415 billion was down 4% year over year with comparable sales off 9%, but adjusted EPS of $2.92 actually beat estimates, helped in part by tariff refunds. What moved the stock was the guidance, not the quarter — full-year revenue is now expected down 5% to 7%, the second downward revision this year, landing a week before Nike veteran Heidi O'Neill takes over as CEO. Morgan Stanley held its Underweight rating and cut its target further; UBS, Deutsche Bank, Bernstein and JPMorgan all trimmed theirs too. Michael Burry, whose largest position is reportedly LULU, called it the “trickster” in his portfolio.

I added to this one Friday — 55 shares through Robinhood into the ••••8840 account, average cost $99.93, inside the day's range and close to the low, after the stock had already gapped down on Thursday night's news. The case isn't that the bad news is wrong — the China softness and North American comp declines are real, and O'Neill is inheriting an actual turnaround, not a paper one. It's that a 17% single-day repricing on a problem the market already knew about (this is the second guidance cut this year, not a surprise from nowhere) tends to front-load more pessimism into the price than the fundamentals alone justify. What would tell me that's wrong: another leg down on real volume once trading resumes Tuesday, or O'Neill's first public comments landing worse than what's already priced in.

The read that matters beyond this one position: a stock can lose almost a fifth of its value in a single session on real, company-specific news, and the index it belongs to can still close the week within 1% of its own high. That's dispersion, not stress — the market pricing an isolated problem as isolated, rather than extrapolating it into something systemic.

Why this matters: with markets shut for Labor Day, nothing trades until Tuesday, so today is a pause, not a signal. What's actually worth watching when trading resumes isn't the index — it's whether LULU's Friday low holds or breaks, since that's the tell for whether Friday's move already found its floor or has another leg.
