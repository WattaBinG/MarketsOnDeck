October 1, 2026Morning Brief

Drafted premarket Thursday, October 1, before the opening bell.

HD's slide deepens as the position grows, and two short-dated calls now race Friday's jobs report

$SPY closed Wednesday, September 30 at $762.59, down 0.21% ($1.61) — a second straight session pulling the index further from the $777.88 closing high it set August 13, now about 2.0% below it. $QQQ held up better, up 0.25% to $739.80, while $DIA lagged hardest of the three, down 0.91% to $508.21 — the same tech-outperforms-Dow pattern that's shown up through most of the past month, just with the Dow's leg wider than usual.

There's no single overnight headline driving this morning. A scan of today's broader financial-news feed turns up mostly single-name analyst notes unrelated to the book (a Scotiabank call on CMS Energy, Jefferies on Teledyne, Goldman trimming its T-Mobile target) rather than anything macro. Crypto is reading the quiet the same way: bitcoin is essentially flat, down 0.06% to $83,825.10, ether up 0.02% to $2,696.80, and solana off 1.49% to $117.69, all as of about 7:31 a.m. ET. With the cash session not yet open, there's no premarket print yet on SPY, QQQ, or DIA to report.

The real test of the week lands tomorrow, not today: the September jobs report is due Friday, October 2 at 8:30 a.m. ET, with consensus looking for roughly 90,000 jobs added, down sharply from August's 162,000, per Kiplinger's economic-calendar preview — a weak print would cement the case for a Fed pause, a strong one would revive October hike talk. That date turns out to matter for the book directly too, not just the index level, which is where this morning's real story is.

Home Depot (HD) in the Trading account is now 100 shares at a $297.03 average cost, up from 75 shares at $300.70 — an add, logged here plainly. It came against a stock that kept sliding: HD closed Wednesday at $284.49, down 1.23% ($3.55) from Tuesday's $288.04, a fresh low below the $289.10 level flagged in this brief Tuesday, leaving the position down about 4.2% from the new average cost. There are also two HD call positions open on top of the shares: 10 contracts at the $287.50 strike expiring October 9 (opened Wednesday) and 2 contracts at the $300 strike expiring tomorrow, October 2 (opened September 23). The $300s are now $15.51 out of the money with one trading day left — a real chance they expire worthless unless HD rallies roughly 5.5% by Friday's close, worth saying plainly rather than leaving out.

TSLA exposure in the book is options-only: 7 calls at the $365 strike, also expiring tomorrow, October 2 (the position was built in pieces starting September 28). TSLA closed Wednesday at $354.81, up 0.56%, leaving the $365s about $10.19 out of the money with the same one-day runway as HD's $300s — both now racing the same Friday close, the same morning the jobs report lands before the bell. TSLA also picked up two real, dated headlines this week: a wrongful-death lawsuit filed in Travis County, Texas today alleges a Model 3 "suddenly and unexpectedly accelerated" before a fatal crash, per Yahoo News' Thursday coverage — Tesla has denied similar unintended-acceleration claims before, and federal safety findings have typically pointed to driver input rather than a vehicle defect in past cases — and separately, the SEC cleared Tesla's board-crafted "auto-vote" proxy mechanism for wider use Tuesday, a governance story that drew criticism from some shareholder activists. Neither is a reason to expect a specific move in the stock; both are real and worth having in view given the options expiring into tomorrow's close.

The Agentic account's Carnival (CCL) call — 1 contract at the $25 strike expiring October 16, opened back on September 2 — is sitting on real, verified gains from this week's news rather than speculation. Carnival reported record third-quarter results Tuesday, including $1.9 billion in net income (an all-time Q3 high) and $7.6 billion in customer deposits (also a record, up 7% year-over-year), and said 2027 is already half-booked at record occupancy and pricing — the stock jumped as much as 13% on the report, per FX Leaders' Wednesday coverage. Morgan Stanley followed Wednesday by raising its price target on the name to $32.50 from $31, keeping an Overweight rating, per Yahoo Finance. CCL itself gave back some of the pop Wednesday, closing at $24.54, down 2.27% ($0.57) from Tuesday's $25.11 — just under the $25 strike. There's currently a sell order queued against the single contract; whatever happens with it will be logged here after the fact, not decided here.

The rest of the book is quieter. SpaceX (SPCX), 153 shares at a $124.25 average cost in the Trading account, closed Wednesday up 1.01% to $150.74, still up about 21.3% on cost; the Agentic account's SPCH, the fund targeting twice SpaceX's daily move, is now down to 4 shares from 8 (average cost unchanged at $8.16, consistent with a partial sale rather than any repricing), and closed at $10.04, up about 23.0% on its remaining cost basis. The only SPCX-adjacent item today is a routine Form 4 ownership-disclosure amendment from insider Antonio Gracias, filed Wednesday — nothing that reads as material. KLA Corporation (KLAC), the Agentic account's other position (2.28 shares at a $189.93 average cost), closed Wednesday down 1.16% to $194.25, still up about 2.3% on cost, with nothing new since Tuesday's AI-backlog story covered here. SoFi (SOFI), 200 shares at a $17.10 average cost, closed at $15.71, down about 8.1% from cost, quiet since Monday's insider Form 144 filing. Quantum Computing (QUBT), 500 shares at an $8.81 average cost, closed at $8.44, down about 4.2% from cost, still shadowed by Monday's disclosure of a self-dealing investigation into company officers — unresolved, with no update today.

Why this matters: tomorrow's jobs report is the real catalyst this week, and it lands at 8:30 a.m. before two short-dated option positions in the book — HD's $300 calls and TSLA's $365 calls — both expire that same afternoon, meaning Friday's number has a direct, dated effect on two live positions rather than just the index level. For the rest of the book, HD's equity add came while the stock kept making fresh lows, and CCL's real earnings-driven gain is the week's one unambiguous win worth noting alongside it — the point of logging both here, win or loss, is the same reason this brief exists at all: a timestamp before the outcome, not after it.

September 30, 2026Morning Brief

Drafted premarket Wednesday, September 30, before the opening bell — and before this morning's PCE report.

PCE day finally arrives, and KLA's AI-backlog number is the one real move in a quiet premarket

$SPY closed Tuesday, September 29 at $764.61, down 0.13% ($1.00) — a second straight loss that leaves the index about 1.7% below the $777.88 closing high it set August 13. $QQQ actually gained, up 0.24% to $738.31, while $DIA slipped in line with SPY, down 0.20% to $512.98 — a split session, quieter than Monday's synchronized decline but the same tech-holds-up-better pattern that's shown up through most of the month.

This morning is the one every recent entry here has been counting down to. The Bureau of Economic Analysis releases the August Personal Consumption Expenditures price index — the Fed's own preferred inflation gauge — at 8:30 a.m. ET, alongside the ADP private-payrolls report and the final read on second-quarter GDP, all landing in the same half hour. Forex-market previews of the release, including BabyPips' event guide, have consensus looking for headline PCE up around 0.4% month-over-month (3.7% year-over-year) and core PCE up 0.3% (3.3% year-over-year) — estimates, not the actual print, since this is being written before the number exists. It's the first real data test of whether the disinflation path holds up against a bond market that spent last week pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to a 19-year high and the 30-year to its highest level since 2004.

Premarket trading is holding almost perfectly still ahead of it: as of about 7:35 a.m. ET, SPY, QQQ, and DIA were each within a few hundredths of a percent of Tuesday's close — a rare morning where none of the three benchmarks has a premarket story of its own, a market waiting rather than reacting. Crypto is reading the same caution instead of diverging from it, which is itself worth noting: bitcoin is down 0.71% to $83,727.01, ether off 1.50% to $2,690.41, and solana down 0.60% to $119.40, all as of about 7:32 a.m. ET — a small, orderly pullback, not the kind of split from equities this brief has flagged on other mornings.

The one genuine mover in the book has nothing to do with this morning's data. KLA Corporation (KLAC), the Agentic account's other position (2.28 shares at a $189.93 average cost), closed up 4.03% Tuesday to $196.53 after the company disclosed its order backlog has surged to $12.57 billion, driven by AI-infrastructure-linked demand for its process-control equipment — the same buildout this brief has tied KLA to before, now showing up as a concrete number rather than a thesis, with analysts framing it as keeping pace with rivals Applied Materials and ASML in the same race. KLAC is down slightly premarket, to about $195.00 as of about 7:34 a.m. ET, putting the position roughly 2.7% above cost — Tuesday's real gain mostly intact even as the stock gives a small piece of it back this morning.

The rest of the Trading account skewed the other way. Home Depot (HD), 75 shares at a $300.70 average cost, had fallen to a 52-week low of $289.10 by mid-afternoon Tuesday, per Investing.com — roughly 30% below its high and enough to push its Relative Strength Index into oversold territory — then kept sliding into the close, finishing the session even lower at $288.04. Analysts are split on what that means: Stifel reiterated a Hold rating on the name this week, while UBS is still at Buy. HD is little-changed premarket at $287.99, leaving the position down about 4.2% from cost, with no company-specific catalyst behind the move beyond the technical level itself.

SoFi (SOFI), 200 shares at a $17.10 average cost also in the Trading account, is still digesting this week's Form 144 filings disclosing insider Kelli Keough's planned RSU-related share sales, flagged in this brief Tuesday — the stock closed at $15.91 and is down further premarket to $15.61, about 8.7% below cost, with no new company-specific news today. Quantum Computing (QUBT), 500 shares at an $8.81 average cost, closed at $8.46 and is little-changed premarket at $8.43, about 4.3% below cost, with no fresh, verified company-specific news today.

SpaceX (SPCX), 153 shares at a $124.25 average cost in the Trading account, and the Agentic account's SPCH, the fund targeting twice SpaceX's daily move (8 shares at an $8.16 average cost), are both holding Monday's post-Starship-orbital-flight gains rather than adding to them this morning — SPCX closed at $149.24 and SPCH at $9.83, both little-changed premarket, up about 20.1% and 20.3% on cost respectively. Nothing new on the company itself since Monday's flight.

The other date on the calendar hasn't moved: the September jobs report is still due Friday, October 2, two trading days out, and will land with the market having just digested whatever this morning's PCE print says about the same inflation path.

Why this matters: today's real story is the number due at 8:30 a.m., not anything premarket is doing ahead of it — a hot PCE print reopens the case for the additional hikes the bond market was already pricing last week, while a soft one would be the first real evidence the disinflation path still holds. For the book specifically, KLA's backlog disclosure is the more durable of this week's two real moves — an actual order number tied to a real capex cycle, not sentiment — worth distinguishing from Home Depot's fresh 52-week low, a name still working through its own housing-cycle headwinds that a single data print can't obviously fix. Neither is a reason to do anything differently with either position this morning; both are simply worth knowing before reading too much into one day's tape.

September 29, 2026Morning Brief

Drafted premarket Tuesday, September 29, before the opening bell.

Starship reaches orbit as a 19-year yield high snaps SPY's winning streak

$SPY closed Monday at $765.52, down 0.76% ($5.83) from Friday's $771.30 — snapping the three-session winning streak flagged in this brief Monday, and pulling the index back to about 1.6% below the $777.88 closing high it set August 13. $QQQ fell harder, down 1.06% to $736.59, while $DIA held up best of the three, down 0.66% to $514.06 — a rare session where all three benchmarks moved the same direction together, rather than the tech-versus-Dow splits that have dominated recent entries here.

The driver wasn't a single headline, it was the bond market extending a move this brief has now tracked for most of the week. The 10-year Treasury yield pushed to 5.24% Monday, its highest level since 2007, and the 30-year touched 5.56%, a level not seen since 2004, per CNBC's Monday coverage — a further climb from the 5.09% 19-year high first flagged here last Thursday, September 24. Boeing added its own drag to the Dow specifically, falling nearly 7% after the FAA said it wouldn't certify the 737 MAX 10 until it assesses a newly disclosed software glitch — a name I don't hold, but real weight in a price-weighted index — while the Iran-driven oil pressure flagged in yesterday's premarket brief carried through the session too, per TheStreet's Monday close coverage.

Crypto is reading the morning differently than yields are — a genuine divergence, not a shrug. Bitcoin is up 1.19% to $84,045.50, ether up 1.84% to $2,717.20, and solana up 0.51% to $119.28, all as of about 7:30 a.m. ET — a risk-on tape in the one asset class that traded straight through Monday's selloff, even as this morning's equity futures are only modestly higher: SPY was up about 0.17% to $766.91, QQQ up 0.33% to $738.97, and DIA up 0.16% to $514.84 as of roughly 7:35 a.m. ET, digesting Monday's losses rather than reversing them.

The open question flagged in this brief yesterday morning is now closed, and it's genuinely mine to report. SpaceX's Starship reached orbit for the first time Monday on its 14th test flight, deploying all 26 of the operational Starlink V3 satellites it carried — both of the mission's primary objectives, achieved. The flight wasn't clean: a single Raptor engine lost power during the initial climb, and SpaceX cut the mission roughly seven hours short of its planned duration, bringing Starship down for a splashdown in the Pacific north of Hawaii instead of running the full profile, per Space.com's and Spaceflight Now's Monday coverage. The company is calling it a success on the objectives that mattered — first orbital flight, first live satellite deployment — while the engine issue is a real, unresolved item for whatever comes next. I hold SpaceX directly through 153 shares at a $124.25 average cost in the Trading account, up about 18.3% on cost as of this morning's $146.95 premarket print; the Agentic account holds SPCH, the fund targeting twice SpaceX's daily move, 8 shares at an $8.16 average cost, up about 16.9% on its own basis at $9.54. SPCX actually fell 2.14% Monday, in line with the broader risk-off tape rather than reacting distinctly to the launch outcome — a reminder that "mission success" and "stock reaction" don't always move together on the same day.

The rest of the book is quieter, with one real dated item. SoFi (SOFI), 200 shares at a $17.10 average cost in the Trading account, fell 3.93% Monday to $15.93 and is only modestly higher premarket at $16.06 — now down about 6.1% from cost. Part of that is the same rate-sensitivity thread this brief has flagged before (a balance-sheet lender's funding costs rising with yields), and part of it is company-specific: an officer, Kelli Keough, filed a second Form 144 this week disclosing plans to sell shares tied to RSU vesting, a roughly $524,000 notice following a similar $1.14 million filing tied to shares that vested September 14, per SEC filings reported by StockTitan. Routine compensation-related selling, not evidence of anything else, but real and dated. Quantum Computing (QUBT), 500 shares at an $8.81 average cost in the Trading account, fell 4.13% Monday to $8.59 and is up modestly premarket to $8.68 — still down about 1.5% from cost, with no fresh company-specific news today; it's still riding the broader quantum-sector strength from IonQ's error-correction milestone (September 23) and Titan Partners' Buy initiation on the name (September 22) earlier this month. Home Depot (HD), 75 shares at a $300.70 average cost, closed Monday at $289.89 and is little-changed premarket at $291.15, down about 3.2% from cost, with no HD-specific news I could confirm today. KLA Corporation (KLAC), the Agentic account's other position (2.28 shares at a $189.93 average cost), closed Monday up 0.57% to $188.99 and is up further premarket to $192.19, putting the position about 1.2% above cost — still the same AI-capex-and-equipment-demand story this brief has covered before, with nothing new specific to today.

Tomorrow is the real test for this week's yield-driven story: the Bureau of Economic Analysis releases the August Personal Consumption Expenditures price index — the Fed's own preferred inflation gauge — Wednesday, September 30 at 8:30 a.m. ET. The September jobs report follows Friday, October 2.

Why this matters: Monday's synchronized decline across SPY, QQQ, and DIA is the bond market still repricing a longer hiking path, the same story this brief has been tracking since last week's Treasury auction — and tomorrow's PCE print is the first real data test of whether the disinflation case the Fed needs still holds up against it. For the book specifically, Starship's first orbital flight is a genuine technical milestone in a position held across both accounts, engine issue and abbreviated mission included — worth knowing the actual outcome landed before the market fully reacts to it, rather than reading too much into one day's premarket tape either way.

Drafted premarket Monday, September 28, before the opening bell — and before Starship's Flight 14 launch window, which opens at 8:15 a.m. ET.

Oil jumps on a rejected Iran deal, and Starship's first orbital shot is minutes from finding out

$SPY closed Friday, September 25 at $771.30, up 0.54% ($4.12) from Thursday's $767.18 — a third straight gain that's pulled the index back to within about 0.8% of the $777.88 closing high it set August 13. $QQQ closed at $744.44, up 0.45%, while $DIA actually led for once, up 0.93% to $517.43 — a reversal of the tech-outperforms-Dow pattern that's dominated most of this month.

That calm didn't survive the weekend. Brent crude jumped more than 4% to above $107 a barrel Monday morning and WTI rose a similar amount to around $96, after President Trump confirmed he'd rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz — a seven-day offer that would have lifted the U.S. naval blockade in exchange for unfreezing Iranian assets and ending the broader conflict "on all fronts," per CNBC's and Al Jazeera's Monday coverage. "They made a proposal but I rejected it," Trump told reporters. Asian shares were broadly softer overnight on the same news, and Treasury yields extended their own recent climb rather than easing — the 30-year touched its highest level since 2004, per Monday market-wrap coverage. The Fed's own calendar hasn't caught up with any of it: the target range still sits at 3.75%-4.00% after this month's hike, the next decision isn't until October 28, and Fed funds futures are pricing roughly a 65% chance of another quarter-point move that day — a separate, later question from this morning's oil spike, not one resolved by it.

Crypto is reading this morning the same risk-off way the oil move suggests, not shrugging it off the way it sometimes has this month: bitcoin is down 2.21% to $83,000.67, ether off 1.69% to $2,663.41, and solana down a sharper 4.30% to $118.58, all as of about 7:30 a.m. ET.

The one event on today's calendar that's actually mine to watch isn't happening on a trading screen. SpaceX's Starship is targeting its first-ever fully orbital launch attempt this morning — Flight 14 — with a 75-minute window opening at 8:15 a.m. ET from Starbase in South Texas, carrying an operational stack of 26 upgraded Starlink V3 satellites, each built with roughly double the prior generation's solar-array power, per Space.com's and CNBC's Monday coverage. Three of those satellites carry cameras specifically to scan the vehicle's heat shield and relay imagery back to engineers working toward eventual return-to-launch-site recovery — a real technical objective for the flight, not just a payload delivery. I hold SpaceX directly through 153 shares at a $124.25 average cost in the Trading account, up about 19.7% on cost as of Friday's $148.73 close; the Agentic account holds SPCH, a fund targeting twice SpaceX's daily move, 8 shares at an $8.16 average cost, up about 19.9% on its own basis at Friday's $9.785. As I'm writing this, the window hasn't opened yet and the outcome isn't known — which is the entire point of logging it here before it happens rather than after.

Nothing else in the book had a confirmed, dated catalyst behind it as of this morning. SoFi (SOFI), 200 shares at a $17.10 average cost in the Trading account, closed Friday at $16.57, down about 3.1% from cost, with no company-specific news I could confirm today. Quantum Computing (QUBT), 500 shares at an $8.81 average cost, also in the Trading account, closed Friday at $8.95, up about 1.6% on cost, still riding the broader quantum-sector strength covered here last week rather than any fresh news of its own. Home Depot (HD), 75 shares at a $300.70 average cost in the Trading account, closed Friday at $293.16, down about 2.5% from cost; a Motley Fool piece published this morning argued the stock's still-soft same-store-sales outlook is a reason to stay away even 32% below its all-time high — commentary, not a new data point, but a real and dated one worth having in view. KLA Corporation (KLAC), the Agentic account's other position (2.28 shares at a $189.93 average cost), closed Friday at $187.91, down about 1.1% from cost, with no fresh company-specific news I could confirm.

Two dates are close enough to flag. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index for August, is now just two trading days out, due Wednesday, September 30. The next jobs report, covering September, follows Friday, October 2 at 8:30 a.m. ET. Both land while the market is still digesting this morning's oil move and an open question about October's rate decision.

Why this matters: today's actual macro story is a geopolitical oil shock landing on top of an already-hawkish rate backdrop — not a reason to expect fireworks in any single stock, but a real reminder that the disinflation path the Fed is counting on assumes energy stays out of the way, and this morning it didn't. For the book specifically, the more interesting test today has nothing to do with rates at all: whether Starship's first fully orbital attempt succeeds, partially succeeds, or doesn't, is a real, unscripted outcome sitting in a name I hold in both accounts, one that'll already be resolved by the time anyone reads this after the fact — which is the entire reason a brief like this gets written before the answer exists, not after.

North America revenue fell 8% (U.S. down 8%, Canada down 11%), and on the earnings call management pointed specifically to "negative commentary across media and social channels" weighing on store and digital traffic in both North America and China.

The guidance cut is what's doing the real damage. Lululemon now expects fiscal Q3 EPS of $0.93 to $0.98 and full-year EPS of $9.48 to $9.73 — down from the $10.95 to $11.15 range it guided to last quarter.

Before reacting to a move like that, the question worth asking is whether it's company-specific or an early read on consumer discretionary broadly. Checking premarket action in Nike (roughly flat), TJX (roughly flat), Deckers (roughly flat), and Abercrombie (roughly flat) points to the former — none of those four are trading in sympathy, which suggests a Lululemon-specific traffic and brand-sentiment problem rather than a sector-wide rotation. That's four names, not a full sector scan, so it's a lean, not a conclusion.

Why this matters: an EPS beat doesn't protect a stock when guidance and comp sales are both moving the wrong direction — the headline number only tells part of the story. And since the peers I checked didn't move with LULU this morning, this reads as a name to actually dig into the earnings call on, not a signal to reposition around anything broader. I'm not adding to or trimming the position this morning; what I'd want to see before deciding either way is whether the North America traffic trend management described actually stabilizes next quarter.
