September 23, 2026Morning Brief

Drafted premarket Wednesday, September 23, before the opening bell.

SPY closed Tuesday, September 22 at $773.50, essentially flat versus Monday's $773.29 (up about 0.03%) — a quiet headline number sitting on top of a real continuation underneath it. The thread connecting overnight action to this morning's premarket is Iran, not chips. Drafted premarket Thursday, September 17, before the opening bell — the morning after the Fed's first rate hike since 2023.

SPY closed Wednesday, September 16 — the day the Federal Reserve did something markets haven't seen in three years — at $754.17, down 0.43% on the session and now about 3.0% below the $777.88 closing high the index set August 13. That headline number actually undersells the day: one layer down, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 631.21 points, or 1.21%, to 51,461.90 — a far sharper drop than SPY's own, on the same piece of news.

The decision itself, when it landed at 2 p.m. ET, was close to exactly what the last several entries here have been building toward: the Federal Open Market Committee voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate by 25 basis points, to a target range of 3.75% to 4.00% — the Fed's first hike since 2023, ending a year that had mostly been defined by rate-cut expectations. What moved the market more than the vote itself was the accompanying dot plot: 16 of the 18 FOMC participants now see at least one more hike before year-end, and four of those see two. Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference ran barely half an hour, but he spent about 22 minutes of it fielding questions, saying inflation has been "too high ... for too long" and that the Fed needs to be "confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective clearly and at sufficient speed" before easing off.

That combination — a hike plus a dot plot pointing toward more — is what actually explains why the Dow underperformed so badly. Big bank stocks had their worst day since February on fears of a longer hiking cycle: Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, Bank of America and Citigroup all fell more than 3%, and with financials carrying real weight in a price-weighted index, that's most of the gap between the Dow's 1.21% drop and SPY's more modest 0.43% decline (DIA, the ETF I track it through, closed at $515.15, down 1.17%). QQQ, meanwhile, actually finished up 0.08% to $705.12 — chip strength (Intel up 4.32% to $101.34, Nvidia up 0.96% to $214.20, AMD up 1.67%) was enough to offset the same rate worry hitting the Nasdaq's own bank-adjacent names. Crypto shrugged the whole thing off: bitcoin down 0.10% to $76,301, ether off 0.07% to $2,432, and solana down 0.26% to $99.62 as of this morning — a quiet, low-drama band that reads as risk appetite holding rather than repricing.

The one piece of Wednesday actually driven by something other than the Fed sits in my own book. SpaceX (SPCX) — 150 shares at a $123.71 average cost in the Trading account (••••8840) — was the biggest gainer among names I hold, up 5.16% to $150.90 on real, company-specific news: Musk confirmed Starship's 14th test flight still targets September 22 from Starbase, its first carrying production Starlink V3 satellites and what CFO Bret Johnsen has called the vehicle's first "revenue-generating" flight. Wednesday's coverage also pointed to SpaceX's rising weight in the next Nasdaq-100 rebalance and a reported new Space Force contract; I'd treat that contract figure as unconfirmed trade-press reporting until it shows up in an actual filing. The position is now up roughly 22.0% on cost. The Agentic account's (••••1117) SPCH, the fund targeting twice SPCX's daily move, tracked it up 9.94% to $10.16 — up about 24.5% on its own $8.16 cost basis.

Quantum Computing (QUBT), 500 shares at an $8.81 average cost in the Trading account, ticked up 1.53% to $7.96 — still down about 9.7% on cost, but on a real if modest catalyst: the company signed a three-year framework agreement Monday with Hamad Bin Khalifa University in Qatar to expand access to its Dirac-3 system, the kind of institutional-adoption deal that matters more for a small-cap name's credibility than for near-term revenue.

SoFi Technologies is the name that ties the bank story directly back to my own book. I've added to the position since it was last mentioned here — now 200 shares at a $17.10 average cost in the Trading account, up from 100 shares previously — and it fell 1.23% Wednesday to $16.86, down about 1.4% on cost. SoFi isn't a money-center bank, but it carries real loan volume on its own balance sheet, so the same fear that hit Goldman and Citigroup Wednesday — a longer, harder hiking cycle raising funding costs — applies here too, in a smaller and less dramatic way. Separately, Citizens Bank has sued SoFi, alleging systematic poaching of its mortgage staff and misuse of trade secrets — a real legal overhang worth having in view, independent of anything rates are doing.

KLA Corporation (KLAC), 2.28 shares at a $189.93 average cost in the Agentic account, was roughly flat Wednesday, down 0.07% to $167.90 and still about 11.6% below cost. I couldn't find any fresh company-specific news behind that — it's the same chip-equipment-and-capex story this brief has flagged before, moving with the sector rather than on its own.

One date worth having on the calendar: the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index for August, is due September 30 — the first real data test of whether the disinflation path the dot plot is now betting against actually holds up.

Why this matters: the Fed hiking rates for the first time in three years, with two-thirds of its own committee penciling in more, is the kind of regime change that doesn't fully price into one session's close — Goldman Sachs research cited in Wednesday's coverage points to the S&P 500 averaging a 2% decline in the months right after a hike, before a roughly 9% gain over the following year. That's a historical pattern, not a forecast for this specific cycle, and it says nothing about any single stock. But it's a useful frame for reading a day like Wednesday's Dow-versus-Nasdaq split, and for the accounts here specifically: SoFi's funding-cost exposure and the semis strength propping up SPCX and QUBT alike are both, in their own way, downstream of the same rate path the Fed just confirmed.
