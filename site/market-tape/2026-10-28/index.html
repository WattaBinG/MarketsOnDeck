Alphabet Burned $9.1 Billion in Cash and Dropped 8.5% After Hours — Microsoft's Azure Grew 48% and the Fed Held at 3.75%–4.00%

First published October 28, 2026, 5:17 PM ET · All After Market Tape entries →

Share X Facebook LinkedIn
October 28, 2026After Market Tape

The Fed gave the market the hold it expected on Wednesday — 3.75%–4.00% in a 10–2 vote — and the after-close earnings split the AI trade in two: Alphabet reported negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion for a second straight quarter and missed on earnings ($2.12 vs about $3.02 expected, FactSet via Barron's), sending the stock down 8.5% after hours, while Microsoft beat on Azure growth of 48% and jumped about 4%.

Recap

At the official closes (Robinhood), SPY rose 0.34% to $772.31, QQQ rose 0.59% to $725.48 and DIA rose 0.27% to $524.68. The S&P 500 index printed 7,743.98 (+0.28% vs Tuesday's 7,722.60) and the Nasdaq-100 printed 29,813.16 (+0.55% vs 29,651.44), with the VIX down 3.48% at 16.91 (Robinhood index quotes); the Dow rose 97.53 points (+0.19%) to 52,475.10 and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.27% to 26,549.85 (WSJ). The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate at 3.75%–4.00% in a 10–2 vote, with Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and Governor Lisa Cook dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike (WSJ); the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.044% (WSJ) and traders priced about an 85% likelihood of a December hike (CME FedWatch via Investopedia). Chips carried the regular session again: the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) rose 2.11% to $521.85, Micron jumped 5.75% to $982.12 after SK Hynix's record quarter, SanDisk rose 7.62% to $1,404.30, Seagate rose 4.49% to $697.83 and Broadcom rose 2.82% to $351.97 (Robinhood closes). Then the bell rang and the verdicts diverged. Microsoft beat — adjusted EPS of $4.89 vs $4.71 expected, revenue of $92.28 billion vs $90.62 billion expected, Azure revenue up 48% — and its shares jumped about 4% after hours (WSJ; Barron's). Alphabet missed — EPS of $2.12 vs about $3.02 expected and revenue of $127.6 billion just shy of the roughly $128 billion call — and, with free cash flow negative for a second straight quarter at -$9.1 billion despite Google Cloud revenue more than doubling to $30.7 billion, its shares fell 8.5% after hours (Barron's; Reuters). Bitcoin's Robinhood evening mark was $81,144.20, holding above $80,000.

Mag 7 on Wednesday, with Alphabet leading the group into its after-close report. Source: Robinhood official closes, October 28, 2026.

The Mag 7 split into the close on Wednesday (Robinhood official closes): Alphabet $325.63 (+1.58%) led the group in the regular session before its after-hours drop. Amazon rose 0.61% to $254.65, Microsoft was nearly flat at $531.73 (+0.08%) before its after-hours jump, Tesla fell 0.74% to $404.46, Nvidia fell 1.17% to $215.39, Meta fell 1.26% to $612.41 and Apple fell 0.93% to $343.86 after unveiling a revamped touchscreen MacBook Pro (TradingView). Elsewhere, earnings movers ran hot: Vertiv soared 16.56% to $243.10 after its morning beat, Oracle jumped 10.63% to $144.40, CoreWeave rose 7.32% to $63.93, Nebius rose 7.10% to $182.57, Etsy gained 5.38% to $92.38, Arm rose 4.14% to $235.97 and KLA rose 3.28% to $158.70, while Robinhood fell 3.33% to $91.41, ServiceNow fell 3.16% to $149.89 ahead of its own after-close report and GE Vernova slipped 1.84% to $878.57 after its morning print (Robinhood closes).

Watch tomorrow: whether Alphabet's after-hours drop and Microsoft's jump hold at Thursday's open, Amazon's earnings after Thursday's close, US GDP and PCE inflation data on Thursday, tonight's after-close reactions from ServiceNow, KLA and T-Mobile, and whether December hike pricing (about 85%, CME FedWatch via Investopedia) survives the data.
