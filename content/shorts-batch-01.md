## Short 1: The Same-Day Win and Loss

**Caption/hook for the post:** Same ticker. Same day. A $1,930 win and a $1,492 loss, hours apart. This is what a real trading account actually looks like.

**[0:00–0:03] Visual Hook:** Hard cut to a split screen — left side shows the -$1,492 fill, right side shows the +$1,930 fill, both timestamped the same day. On-screen text: **"SAME DAY. SAME TICKER."**

**[0:03–0:15] Problem Statement — spoken narration:**
"Every trading account you see online is either all wins or a sob story. Mine isn't either. August 7th, SOXL — I lost $1,492 on one position and made $1,930 on another, same day."
On-screen text overlay: "SOXL — Aug 7, 2026"

**[0:15–0:50] On-Screen Solution/Demo — spoken narration, timestamped beats:**
- **[0:15–0:25]** "Here's the loss first, because that's not the part people usually show." [Show the -$1,492 fill, brokerage record, account number blurred.] On-screen text: "-$1,492"

## Under the Hood (Internal Note — do not publish)

Both shorts lead with the loss or the tension, not the win — that's deliberate. A short that opens with a win reads as bragging; one that opens with a loss and resolves it (or doesn't) reads as documentation, which is the entire brand promise. These are also the two moments already flagged as the strongest "process vs. outcome" material in the real data — reusing them here means the shorts and Episode 1 reinforce each other instead of competing for the same story.
